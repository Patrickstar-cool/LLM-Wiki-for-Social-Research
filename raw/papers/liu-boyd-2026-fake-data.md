# The State's Politics of "Fake Data"

**Authors:** Chuncheng Liu (Northeastern University), danah boyd (Cornell University)

**Venue:** CHI '26 (ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems), April 13–17, 2026, Barcelona

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790583

## Abstract

Data have power. As such, most discussions of data presume that records should mirror some idealized ground truth. Deviations are viewed as failure. Drawing on two ethnographic studies of state data-making—in a Chinese street-level bureaucrat agency and at the US Census Bureau—we show how seemingly "fake" state data perform institutional work. We map four moments in which actors negotiate between representational accuracy and organizational imperatives: creation, correction, collusion, and augmentation. Bureaucrats routinely privilege what data do over what they represent, creating fictions that serve civil servants' self-interest and enable constrained administrations. We argue that "fakeness" of state data is relational (context dependent), processual (emerging through workflows), and performative (brought into being through labeling and practice). We urge practitioners to center fitness-for-purpose in assessments of data and contextual governance. Rather than chasing impossible representational accuracy, sociotechnical systems should render the politics of useful fictions visible, contestable, and accountable.

## Key Concepts

### Theoretical Framework
- **Relational fakeness**: Whether data are taken as real or fake depends on actors' positionality in specific contexts and power relationships
- **Processual fakeness**: Must be understood within the state's organizational structure of data production pipeline
- **Performative fakeness**: Labeling data as "fake" is a political act that brings certain realities into being

### Four Moments of Data Fakeness
1. **Creation**: Street-level bureaucrats fabricate or manipulate data to meet quotas — ranging from outright fabrication to "hybrid data" blending real events with targeted alterations
2. **Correction**: Mid-level validation that realigns data with bureaucratic expectations rather than objective reality
3. **Collusion**: Coordinated acceptance of fake data between hierarchical levels when it serves institutional needs ("fake something that is more convincing!")
4. **Augmentation**: Active injection of fakeness through statistical processing (imputation, age-smoothing) that makes individual records "faker" but aggregate portraits "realer"

### Empirical Cases
- **China**: Street-level bureaucrats documenting community volunteering in "Meritown" — 10 months ethnography, 108 interviews
- **US**: Census Bureau's 2020 census data production — 4-year ethnography, 103 interviews

### Design Implications
- Contextual data governance: fitness-for-purpose standards instead of universal accuracy thresholds
- Uncertainty-forward system design: foreground provenance, confidence measures, preserve dissent
- "All data are fake, but some are useful" — pragmatic recognition rather than cynical nihilism
