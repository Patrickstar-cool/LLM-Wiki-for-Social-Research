# Working towards a dialectical understanding of the political ideology within technological projects

**Author:** Frederick Reiber (Boston University)

**Venue:** CHIdeology Workshop 2026, CHI '26, Barcelona

**Type:** Position paper

## Abstract

This position paper develops a framework for analyzing the politics of technological projects, drawing on models from dialectical reasoning and critical social science. Rather than treating politics as something external to design, the author argues that political commitments should be understood as internal to technological work itself. The framework is illustrated through an analysis of the Mechanism Design for Social Good (MD4SG) research agenda.

## Key Concepts

### Three-Component Definition of Ideology

Drawing on Burawoy's Marxist sociology framework (the tension between utopian imagination and anti-utopian science), Reiber defines a project's ideology through three guiding questions:

1. **Values**: Which normative commitments are prioritized, and in what order?
2. **Constraints**: Which threats/limitations are recognized as legitimate or "real"?
3. **Intervention**: How does the project synthesize values and constraints into a theory of change? What form does the proposed action take?

### Case Study: MD4SG
- **Values**: Efficiency, access, opportunity — improvement within existing systems of allocation and exchange
- **Constraints**: Incentive compatibility (human action = competition), hegemonic economic regimes accepted as given (no engagement with colonialism, structural inequality)
- **Intervention**: Technological development as primary element of change — algorithms, SMS services, mechanism design — not structural transformation or collective action

### The Dialectical Move
The key analytical insight: disentangling ideology requires treating values, constraints, and interventions as a dialectical whole, not as separable components. The choice of which constraints to recognize as valid is itself ideological.
