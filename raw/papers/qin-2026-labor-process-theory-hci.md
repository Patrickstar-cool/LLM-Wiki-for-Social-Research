# Labor, Capital, and Machine: Toward a Labor Process Theory for HCI

**Authors:** Yigang Qin and EunJeong Cheon

**Date:** February 2026 | **Accepted to CHI'26**

**DOI:** 10.1145/3772318.3791147

**Subjects:** Human-Computer Interaction (cs.HC)

**License:** CC BY 4.0

## Abstract

The HCI community has called for renewed attention to labor issues and the political economy of computing. Yet much work remains in engaging with labor theory to better understand modern work and workers. This article traces the development of Labor Process Theory (LPT)—from Karl Marx and Harry Braverman to Michael Burawoy and beyond—and introduces it as an essential yet underutilized resource for structural analysis of work under capitalism and the design of computing systems.

## Labor Process Theory (LPT) Genealogy

### Marx
- Capitalism extracts **surplus value** at the point of production
- Workers sell labor power to capitalists
- The gap between what workers produce and their wages = exploitation
- "Structured antagonism" between labor and capital

### Harry Braverman (1974, *Labor and Monopoly Capital*)
- Deskilling thesis: capitalism systematically degrades work
- Separation of conception from execution
- Scientific management (Taylorism) as control mechanism

### Michael Burawoy (1979, *Manufacturing Consent*)
- Why do workers work hard? Not just coercion — **consent**
- "Making out" games on the shop floor
- Ideological mechanisms that mask exploitation

### Contemporary LPT
- Post-Fordism, lean production, platformization
- Emotional labor, aesthetic labor
- Algorithmic management
- Global labor arbitrage

## Seven Directions for HCI Research

1. **Distinguish labor from work** — labor is the activity under capitalist relations; work is the concrete practice
2. **Link work practice to value production** — how does design contribute to surplus extraction?
3. **Study up the management** — not just workers, but managers and capital
4. **Analyze consent and legitimacy** — how do technologies manufacture worker consent?
5. **Move beyond the point of production** — social reproduction, domestic labor, global supply chains
6. **Design alternative institutions** — platform cooperatives, commons-based models
7. **Unnaturalize bourgeois designs** — "productivity" and "efficiency" are not neutral; they serve capital

## Relevance to Social Science
- Bridges Marxist political economy with HCI/design research
- Extends critical theory tradition into technology studies
- Reframes "user-centered design" as potentially serving capital, not workers
- Connects to labor sociology, critical management studies, platform studies
