# Gendered Communication Patterns of Political Elites on Truth Social

**Authors:** Tom Bidewell, Artemis Deligianni, Tuğrulcan Elmas, Clare Llewellyn, Björn Ross (University of Edinburgh)

**Venue:** 18th ACM Web Science Conference (WebSci '26), May 26–29, 2026, Braunschweig, Germany

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3795766.3799762

## Abstract

The influence of gender on online political communication remains contested. This paper addresses the gap in understanding gendered dynamics on alt-tech social media by presenting the first large-scale analysis of political elite communication on Truth Social, using a novel dataset of 107k posts from 129 U.S. political figures. The study finds that many gendered communication patterns documented on mainstream platforms persist on Truth Social: women political elites express more joy and less anger than men, and receive significantly higher levels of audience engagement. At the same time, nuanced differences emerge: although men and women discuss largely similar conservative themes, posts by women political elites contain higher levels of fear-based rhetoric, potentially suggesting selective adaptation to navigate gender norms on the platform.

## Key Findings

### RQ1: Content Impact of Gender
- Men and women discuss largely similar conservative themes
- Differences emerge in how issues are framed and in rhetorical strategies employed

### RQ2: Rhetorical Style
- Women express significantly more joy and less anger than men
- Women's posts contain higher levels of fear-based rhetoric — a finding specific to this alt-tech platform

### RQ3: Audience Engagement
- Women political elites receive significantly higher levels of audience engagement (upvotes, replies)
- This mirrors patterns found on mainstream platforms

### Platform Context
- Truth Social = hyper-partisan, unmoderated, associated with hegemonic masculinity
- User base: strong Trump supporters, correlated with traditionalist gender role views
- Platform functions as centralized "mouthpiece" for Donald Trump
- Critical: alt-tech platforms have historically shifted from passive discourse to active mobilization (Jan 6)

### Theoretical Significance
- Expands gendered political communication literature beyond mainstream platforms
- Demonstrates that even in ideologically homogeneous, hyper-masculine online spaces, gendered communication patterns persist
- Suggests women political elites strategically adapt communicative styles to navigate gender norms
