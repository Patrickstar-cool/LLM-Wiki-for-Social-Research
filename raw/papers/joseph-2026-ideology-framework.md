# A conceptual framework for ideology beyond the left and right

**Authors:** Kenneth Joseph (University at Buffalo), Kim Williams (Portland State University), David Lazer (Northeastern University)

**Date:** March 19, 2026

**Subjects:** Computers and Society (cs.CY); Computation and Language (cs.CL)

**License:** CC BY 4.0

## Abstract

NLP+CSS work has operationalized ideology almost exclusively on a left/right partisan axis. This approach obscures the fact that people hold interpretations of many different complex and more specific ideologies on issues like race, climate, and gender. We introduce a framework that understands ideology as an attributed, multi-level socio-cognitive concept network, and explains how ideology manifests in discourse in relation to other relevant social processes like framing. We demonstrate how this framework can clarify overlaps between existing NLP tasks (e.g. stance detection and natural language inference) and also how it reveals new research directions. Our work provides a unique and important bridge between computational methods and ideology theory, enabling richer analysis of social discourse in a way that benefits both fields.

## Key Contributions

1. **Framework:** Ideology as an attributed, multi-level socio-cognitive concept network
2. **Bridge:** Connects ideology theory (from sociology, political science, psychology) with NLP+CSS methods
3. **New directions:** Computational detection of ideologies beyond left/right, measuring downstream impacts
4. **Unifying lens:** Shows how stance detection, framing, NLI connect through ideology

## Core Framework

### Multi-level network of concepts
- Ideologies are shared and coherent worldviews that explain "why it is how it is" and "what should be done about it"
- They are organized as networks of interconnected concepts (not just a single dimension)
- Example: colorblind racial ideology contains concepts like "abstract liberalism", "minimization of racism"

### Three challenges
1. Identifying which ideologies *exist*
2. Identifying the *contents* of a particular ideology (in a given socio-temporal context)
3. Determining which discursive patterns represent *adherence* to which ideologies

### Ideology vs. related constructs
- **Framing:** How issues are presented in discourse (related but distinct from ideology)
- **Stance detection:** Position on a specific issue (a surface manifestation of ideology)
- **NLI (Natural Language Inference):** Can detect entailment relationships between ideological concepts

## Key Ideologies Discussed
- **Colorblind racial ideology** (Bonilla-Silva): U.S. is "post-racial", abstract liberalism
- **Intersectional ideology** (Collins, Crenshaw): Identity is multifaceted, structural conditions cause inequality
- Both are contested, fluid, and evolve over time

## Relevance to Social Science
- Bridges the gap between computational methods and social theory
- Moves beyond simplistic left/right spectrum
- Enables study of ideology in non-Western contexts
- Can be applied to LLM bias detection
